# APU Cloud Innovation Hackathon 2026 brief

What the APU Cloud Innovation Hackathon 2026 asks for: who can enter, what to build, what to hand in, and by when. It condenses the website, Discord, and briefing captures in [docs/sources](/docs/sources/) as they stood on 3 November 2026 at 8:30 PM, after the briefing, citing website pages by name, Discord channels by `#channel`, and the recorded briefing as [briefing]. Where the sources disagree, both readings are given.

## At a glance

- **Theme:** "When Cloud, IoT, and Network Converge": build an idea that brings the three together. Cloud computes beyond limits, IoT senses the world, and the network connects it all ([home page]).
- **When:** 26 October – 17 November 2026 ([home page]).
- **Format:** the build phase is online. Final pitching for the top five teams is currently planned for 17 November at the APU Auditorium during NexConnect '26 ([#faq-and-guidelines], [#key-dates-and-briefing]).
- **Organisers:** APU School of Technology, with GDGoC APU supporting coordination and execution ([event details]). The site lists NexConnect 2026, NTT DATA (sponsor), and Google Cloud as partners ([community]).
- **Tools:** the organisers encourage Google tools such as Gemini Enterprise where they fit, and welcome others; the NTT DATA presenters at the briefing said Gemini Enterprise is mandatory (see [Tools](#tools)).
- **Prizes:** RM4,000 in cash across five places ([home page]).
- **Cost:** free ([#faq-and-guidelines]).

## Eligibility and teams

- Only current APU students can take part; students from other universities or colleges are not eligible. On 30 October the organisers corrected earlier wording that suggested otherwise ([#announcements]).
- Teams have 2–5 members including the leader, all current APU students. Solo participation and solo submissions are not accepted ([event details], [#announcements]).
- Every member registers individually: sign in to the team workspace with your own account and add your participant details, entering APU details accurately. Then sign in again to create or join a team ([event details], [team workspace], [#faq-and-guidelines]).
- Sign in with Google or email. An emailed sign-in link must be opened in the same browser on the same device; use your own email, not a shared team account ([team workspace]).
- One leader creates the team and shares its private invite link; the others join through it ([#team-formation]). If two teammates each create a team, both become leaders and neither can join the other's: on 2 November an organiser had to delete both teams so they could start again ([#general]).
- To correct a registration, ask in `#help-desk` before registering again, so organisers can avoid duplicate teams. The process for changing members or challenge after registering has not been announced; ask an organiser before making changes ([#faq-and-guidelines]).
- Keep phone numbers, email addresses, resumes, sign-in links, invite links, and project files out of public channels ([#team-formation], [#submission-guidelines], [team workspace]).

## Challenge tracks

Each team chooses exactly one track. The team leader selects it in the workspace; a Discord challenge tag does not register the choice ([#problem-statements], [#faq-and-guidelines]). The listed ideas are starting points, not required features, but the project should address the chosen track ([home page], [#faq-and-guidelines]). At the briefing the presenters repeated that the examples are only a start and asked teams to bring their own ideas ([briefing]).

- **Smart Campus & Student Experience:** make campus life more useful, accessible, and engaging. Website ideas: finding and accessing campus services, more accessible student experiences, connecting students with campus resources ([home page]). Discord ideas: campus navigation, personalised onboarding, an AI learning companion ([#problem-statements]).
- **Intelligent Automation & Digital Transformation:** bring AI into everyday workflows and streamline repetitive university work. Website ideas: automating repetitive campus workflows, connecting devices with useful actions, making university processes easier to use ([home page]). Discord ideas: HR/IT support, document processing, student performance insights for lecturers ([#problem-statements]).
- **Data-Driven Research & Intelligent Decision Making:** connect data with real questions to help people make better decisions. Website ideas: turning research data into useful insights, exploring patterns in data, supporting evidence-based decisions ([home page]). Discord ideas: research visualisation, trend analysis, resource forecasting ([#problem-statements]).

## Tools

What the organisers posted on 3 November:

- Use Google tools and services where they fit your idea, for example Gemini, Gemini Enterprise, Antigravity, and Google Cloud. Other tools are welcome too; if unsure, ask in `#help-desk` ([#announcements]).
- Teams are encouraged to use Gemini Enterprise "where it fits", and Google will support participants with trial licences for it ([#project-discussion]).
- Google Cloud credits are still being confirmed; the organisers will post an update ([#announcements], [#project-discussion]).

What the NTT DATA presenters said at the briefing ([briefing Q&A]):

- Gemini Enterprise is mandatory. Agents should be built inside Gemini Enterprise, with Antigravity as a supporting tool, for example to connect third-party (3P) connectors and MCP servers. At another point the presenter called both Gemini Enterprise and Antigravity the "required prerequisite", and asked teams to show how they used them and what came of it ([briefing]).
- Connectors are not limited to Google ones: MCP servers and third-party services such as Salesforce, SAP, and Jira, many with free sandboxes, are welcome ([briefing]).
- Gemini Enterprise is low-code and no-code, so code is not required ([briefing Q&A]).
- Google sponsors a Gemini Enterprise trial licence for the hackathon; whether it is per person or per team was not known. To use it, sign in to the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/) with the account the licence is allocated to, search for Gemini Enterprise, create an application, then turn on chat agents and workflows under **Configuration** → **Feature management** ([briefing]).
- If the console offers a free trial or asks you to buy credits instead, your licence may not have been allocated yet. Send access questions to the organisers, who collect them for NTT DATA ([briefing Q&A]).
- The presenter was not sure about Google Cloud credits and pointed to free data options instead, such as Supabase, SharePoint, or Google Workspace ([briefing]).

Earlier posts are looser: the welcome post says to "build a solution using Google AI tools" ([#welcome-and-rules]), and the FAQ says no required tech stack or experience level is listed on the website ([#faq-and-guidelines]). See [Source discrepancies](#source-discrepancies).

## Submission requirements

The team leader submits a working prototype through the team workspace, with ([event details], [#submission-guidelines]):

- A project title
- One selected challenge track
- A summary of at least 50 characters
- An HTTPS link to the code repository
- A PDF pitch deck of up to 10 MB
- A demo link: optional on the website and in `#faq-and-guidelines`, listed without "optional" in `#submission-guidelines`

Only the leader can edit and submit the final entry. Saving a draft is not a final submission, and final submission locks the project and the PDF ([event details], [#submission-guidelines]).

At the briefing, organiser Cindy Pua confirmed that the requirements are the ones listed on the website and Discord: a GitHub repository link, slides, and a demo link. In the call chat she wrote that teams submit the repository link "along with your deployed app or website link" through the official submission portal, and that the submission link and instructions will be shared on Discord once submissions open ([briefing Q&A]).

The NTT DATA presenters asked for more ([briefing Q&A]):

- A structured README in the repository covering five things: the existing problem, how you solve it, the research you did, how the team worked, and the impact or outcome.
- Slides or a PDF and a high-level diagram in the repository; NotebookLM can generate slides. Code is not required, and making the repository public is up to you.
- A three-to-four-minute video covering the same five points and showing how the solution works. Earlier in the session the presenter had described a ten-minute video or a report in Git ([briefing]).
- Asked whether a website is expected, the presenter said reviewers such as himself (Deepanraj Vellingiri) or Sushanth Shetty will be invited to the team's Gemini Enterprise app, whose URL is private to the project.

On 3 November a participant on a five-member team reported that the workspace would not let them edit the project title, challenge, or other fields; no answer had been posted at capture time ([#general]).

## Judging and prizes

The website still says judging criteria "will be released soon" and to follow the community channels ([event details]); no official criteria had been posted as of 3 November. Asked at the briefing, the NTT DATA presenter said an NTT DATA team will review and rate the projects on ([briefing Q&A]):

- How the solution affects students, the university, or operations
- How it was built technically, mainly the first-party (1P) and third-party (3P) connectors used, with MCP as an add-on
- How well the team researched the problem
- How soon the impact arrives, for example three or six months
- For automated workflows, how they work in day-to-day use

The website names no speakers or judges ([community]). The briefing slides list the NTT DATA team that ran it: Udit Agarwal (Head of Cloud and AI), Sushanth Shetty, Aditya Thorat, and Deepanraj Vellingiri (FDEs), and Eelyn Yong and Nabila Zunaidi (SDE-1) ([briefing slides]). The presenter did not say who on the NTT DATA side will review.

## Source discrepancies

- **Gemini Enterprise:** mandatory, with agents built inside it, according to the NTT DATA presenters, who called Antigravity both a "required prerequisite" and a supporting tool ([briefing Q&A], [briefing]); encouraged "where it fits", with other tools welcome, in `#announcements` and `#project-discussion`. Earlier, `#welcome-and-rules` said to build "using Google AI tools" and `#faq-and-guidelines` said no required tech stack is listed ([Discord capture]).
- **Deliverables:** the NTT DATA presenters asked for a README covering five points and a three-to-four-minute video ([briefing Q&A]); the website and `#submission-guidelines` ask for a repository link, a PDF pitch deck, and a demo link, with no video. Organiser Cindy Pua pointed participants to the website and Discord list ([briefing Q&A]).
- **Demo link:** optional on the website, in `#faq-and-guidelines`, and on the briefing's slide 6 ([Discord capture], [briefing slides]); listed without "optional" in `#submission-guidelines` ([Discord capture]); a "deployed app or website link" in the organiser's briefing chat ([briefing Q&A]).
- **Solo entries:** the website requires 2–5 members and `#announcements` rules out solo entries ([event details], [#announcements]); the NTT DATA presenter mentioned individual entries in passing when describing the README ([briefing]).
