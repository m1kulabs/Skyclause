## This hackathon's lineage

No earlier edition was found. This looks like the first APU Cloud Innovation Hackathon, and no NexConnect hackathon with published winners turned up.

- The GDGoC APU chapter's completed-events list on gdg.community.dev holds 24 events, from 18 October 2024 to 28 October 2026. All of them are workshops or talks, and none is a hackathon ([chapter events]).
- Not found: any earlier "APU Cloud Innovation Hackathon" or NexConnect hackathon. Searched apu.edu.my, gdg.community.dev, the GDGoC APU Instagram account (about 150 posts), Brave, Exa and web search. Apart from this year's site, "NexConnect" only matched unrelated companies. The root domain gdgocapu.com did not resolve on 3 November 2026.

The same student club and the same school have run related events:

- **GDSC APU Google Workspace Hackathon 2024** (1 July – 24 August 2024). The brief was to "create innovative solution suites tailored for small businesses using Google Workspace and Apps Script", across five case studies (sales, HR, finance, marketing, operations). The top 8 shared RM8,500. Final judges included Google Cloud's Director of Strategic Pursuit (Public Sector, ASEAN) and APU's Chief Innovation & Enterprise Officer ([workspace site]). Final judging covered "technical, pitch, and business solution" ([workspace criteria], secondary). The winners were Gulugulu Yestion (champion), Marinated Portobello Mushroom Burger with Cheese (1st runner-up) and Wantanmee (2nd runner-up), plus five consolation teams ([workspace champion], [workspace runner-up], [workspace 2nd], secondary). The champion's project was EzBiznes, "an all-in-one hub for SMEs to streamline their operations" ([ezbiznes], secondary). The account's bio still promises a 2025 edition, but its last post is dated 24 August 2024.
- **MY Streamlit Hack** (8 September – 6 October 2023, Devpost, supported by GDSC @ APU and other APU clubs). Teams built "a data science solution and host it on Streamlit", judged on "Innovative Idea" and "Workable Solution". It drew 93 participants. Winners not verified ([streamlit hack]).
- **Maybank Hackathon 2025.** Organised by staff and students of APU's School of Technology, which also organises this hackathon. Held on 22 November 2025, with the final on 8 December 2025. Maybank staff judged on "innovation, technical feasibility, business value, real-world applicability, presentation quality, and teamwork". The champion, Byte-Sized, built a DevSecOps assistant that pulls together findings from code analysis, dependency scanning and infrastructure checks, then ranks the most critical risks. School of Technology students took all three podium places ([maybank]).
- **Great Malaysia AI Hackathon 2025.** Hosted by APU's School of Computing with AWS and MDEC: 1,547 students and 194 professionals, more than 300 teams. APU's Ctrl + Chill won with MySelamat, a flood-rescue system that integrates "real-time, crowdsourced social media data with AI-powered rescue route planning using the Google Maps API" ([myselamat]).

## Malaysian student hackathons

### KitaHack

KitaHack is GDG on Campus Malaysia's nationwide hackathon on the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), and it serves as a lead-in to the Google Solution Challenge. The 2026 site says it launched in 2021 ([kitahack 2026]); Monash says it was "established in 2020" ([kitahack 2024]).

- **2023:** winners not found.
- **2024:** the final was on 28 January 2024. About 60 teams entered and 10 reached the final. Awards went by category: Watt's Up Wizards (best impact), FourFuture (best pitch) and Bazoot (best technical). Their projects were not found ([kitahack 2024]).
- **2025:** 201 teams (603 participants) registered and 109 teams submitted, from which a top 10 was picked ([kitahack 2025 stats], secondary). Demo Day was on 4 May 2025 at Monash. The champion was EqualLens, "an AI-powered recruitment platform tackling bias and inefficiency in hiring". Judges were "impressed by the real-world relevance, seamless automation, and commitment to fairness and equal opportunity". The 1st runner-up was Hokkien Mee is Red, the 2nd runner-up The Green Reapers, and the special award went to KatakHack ([kitahack 2025]). EqualLens was built by Universiti Malaya students ([kitahack 2026 speakers]). Hokkien Mee is Red built Medimate, a healthcare management assistant for SDG 3.8 ([medimate], secondary). The Green Reapers' and KatakHack's projects were not found. Two teams with APU members reached the top 10: iPocket ([ipocket], secondary) and ZeroWasteChef by team AMU, a mix of APU and MMU students ([zerowastechef], secondary).
- **2026:** 1,801 participants in 636 teams from 70 universities ([kitahack 2026 scale], secondary). The organisers' placings were 1st Ice Breakers, 2nd sudo rm -rf / ("technical depth and execution"), 3rd Airost and a special award for GoogleGaga ([kh26 1st], [kh26 2nd], [kh26 3rd], [kh26 special], secondary). Airost (UTM) built TrustLens, which checks 11 document types with up to 46 automated risk checks. It explains its findings through a RAG assistant, can hand cases to forensic experts, and has a community "Alert Zone" ([trustlens], secondary). GoogleGaga (Monash) built OSCE Partner, where medical students practise clinical exams on an AI patient using the Gemini 2.5 Flash Live API, Firebase and MediaPipe hand tracking ([osce partner], secondary). Not found: what Ice Breakers and sudo rm -rf / built.
- **Judging format:** before KitaHack 2026, the 2025 champion ran a workshop on "preliminary & final round judging criteria" and "tips for strong submissions (demo video, documentation & code)" ([kh26 workshop], secondary). That suggests the preliminary round is judged on the submitted video, documentation and code, much like this hackathon's asynchronous round.

## Global comparables

### Google Solution Challenge

The winners page lists the top 10 and winners for each year ([sc winners]). Winners by year:

- **2022:** Blossom (menstrual education), Gateway (an IoT COVID check-in gate that talks to an embedded device over Bluetooth), Xtrinsic (mental-health research and therapy using a wearable), and Zero-zone (lip-reading practice for people with hearing impairments).
- **2023:** Wonder Reader (a 3D-printed braille device), BuzzBusters (early warning for mosquito-borne disease) and HeadHome (dementia wandering). FarmX won People's Choice ([sc 2023]).
- **2024:** ATTI (a voice assistant for mild dementia), Spoon Share (food redistribution) and Alpha-Eye (early detection of eye disease). Saheli, a women's safety app, won People's Choice ([sc 2024]).
- **2025:** the challenge was run by region (see [above](#malaysian-teams-in-the-solution-challenge)). In the APAC edition, Medicall by team Atempo (Konkuk University) took a top-3 place and the Most Societal Impact Award. Medicall is "an AI-powered Emergency Room Matching System connecting paramedics to the nearest available hospital" ([medicall]).
- **2021 and earlier:** not covered. The winners page text retrieved stops at 2022.

The competition ran from 15 May to 12 August 2024 ([gemini winners]). The category winners were:

- Best overall: Jayu, an on-screen personal assistant.
- Most impactful and People's Choice: VITE VERE, which supports people with cognitive disabilities in daily life.
- Most useful and best Flutter app: Prospera, real-time coaching during sales conversations.
- Most creative: Outdraw AI.
- Best Android app: Gaze Link, which lets ALS patients communicate with their eyes.
- Best ARCore app: Everies.
- Best Firebase app: Trippy, a trip planner.

The hackathon ran from 12 May to 23 June 2025 and drew more than 10,400 participants from 62 countries, who submitted 477 projects ([adk winners]):

- **Grand prize:** SalesShortcut, a multi-agent sales development representative that finds leads, researches them and drafts outreach.
- **Asia Pacific winner:** GreenOps, which "continuously audits, forecasts, and optimizes cloud infrastructure" for sustainability.
- **Other regional winners:** Edu.AI (essay grading and study plans for Brazil), Energy Agent AI and Nexora-AI.

## Patterns among winners

These patterns come from the winners above. The counts cover the 10 Solution Challenge winners from 2022 to 2024 (People's Choice excluded).

- **A narrow, named user with an urgent problem.** Winners name a specific group rather than "students" or "users": people with mild dementia (ATTI), dementia patients who wander (HeadHome), ALS patients (Gaze Link), blind students and their teachers (Wonder Reader), medical students preparing for clinical exams (OSCE Partner), flood rescuers (MySelamat).
- **Health and accessibility dominate the global top spots.** Nine of the 10 Solution Challenge winners address health (including public health) or disability. Spoon Share (food) is the only exception ([sc winners], [sc 2023], [sc 2024]). In the Gemini competition, three winners serve disabled users: Gaze Link, VITE VERE and ViddyScribe ([gemini winners]).
- **Dedicated hardware is rare, but it shows up among winners.** Three of the 10 Solution Challenge winners built a device: Gateway (an embedded check-in unit over Bluetooth), Wonder Reader (a 3D-printed braille display over Bluetooth) and Xtrinsic (a wearable). Others used phone sensors, such as the camera in Gaze Link and OSCE Partner. None of the KitaHack 2025–2026 podium projects with public details documents any hardware.
- **Several Google products in one pipeline, not a lone chatbot.** EqualLens chains Gemini, Document AI, Cloud Vision, Speech-to-Text and Firebase ([equallens]). BuzzBusters lists seven Google tools. Seven of the 10 Solution Challenge winners used both Flutter and Firebase.
- **Trust and human-in-the-loop features.** TrustLens offers expert review and a community alert zone ([trustlens], secondary). MySelamat uses crowdsourced verification, and its lead said every AI feature was meant "to strengthen human decision-making" ([myselamat]). EqualLens detects biased wording in job posts and censors personal data in transcripts ([equallens]). Spoon Share has organisations approve food listings ([sc 2024]).
- **Real-world fit is what judges name.** KitaHack 2025 judges cited EqualLens's "real-world relevance" ([kitahack 2025]). Maybank judged "real-world applicability" and "business value" ([maybank]). The GDSC APU Workspace Hackathon judged a "business solution" ([workspace criteria], secondary).
- **Measured validation is rare, and it stands out where it exists.** Gaze Link reports tests with 30 people, up to 85% fewer keystrokes, and "7x more effective than traditional E-transfer boards" ([gaze link]). Wonder Reader thanked Mitra Netra, a support group for blind people ([wonder reader]). Most other write-ups give no user-testing figures.
- **A demo video is standard.** Each 2023 Solution Challenge winner links a demo video ([sc 2023]), and KitaHack preliminaries appear to judge video, documentation and code ([kh26 workshop], secondary).

Local signals point the same way:

- The hackathon's own problem statements suggest campus navigation, personalised onboarding, an AI learning companion, HR/IT support, document processing and student performance insights ([problem statements]). Many teams are likely to start from this list (inference).
- GDGoC APU's recent workshops taught the same patterns: "AI Attendance Tracking & Face Recognition with YOLO" (March 2025), "Beyond the Chatbot: Building AI Agents with Google Genkit" (October 2026) and a vision-assistive AI app (October 2026) ([chapter events]). ### How winners in crowded categories stood out

- **Recruitment** (464 resume-screening matches): EqualLens covered the whole hiring process rather than CV ranking alone. It flags biased wording in job posts, extracts CVs with Document AI, verifies candidates' IDs, and transcribes interviews with personal data removed ([equallens]).
- **Document processing:** TrustLens set out concrete scope (11 document types, 46 checks) and added explanations, expert escalation and a shared alert zone ([trustlens], secondary).
- **Dementia:** HeadHome narrowed to wandering and community help ([sc 2023]). ATTI narrowed to recalling life events and managing routines ([sc 2024]).
- **Food waste** (5,171 matches): Spoon Share made it a two-sided map, with listings that organisations or volunteers approve before they appear ([sc 2024]).
- **Medical education chatbots:** OSCE Partner used real-time voice and hand tracking to rehearse a physical exam, not a text chat ([osce partner], secondary).
- **Assistive communication:** Gaze Link backed its claim with a 30-person test ([gaze link]).
- **Floods** (2,919 `flood` matches): MySelamat combined live crowdsourced social media data with risk-aware routing in Google Maps ([myselamat]).

## Takeaways for this hackathon

Each item separates what the evidence shows from what we infer for a one-week build that is first assessed asynchronously from the repo and deck.

- **Evidence:** this is the first edition, so there is no record of past winners. The nearest signals come from the same organisers: the School of Technology's Maybank criteria (innovation, feasibility, business value, real-world applicability, presentation, teamwork) and GDSC APU's 2024 criteria (technical, pitch, business). **Inference:** until the official rubric is released, plan for feasibility and business value to count as much as technical depth. See [judging and pitching](/docs/research/judging-and-pitching.md).
- **Evidence:** winners name a narrow user with an urgent problem. **Inference:** pick one specific APU group, such as facilities staff, lab technicians, lecturers, researchers or IT support, rather than "students".
- **Evidence:** three of the 10 Solution Challenge winners built hardware, and no KitaHack podium project with public details did. **Inference:** with a theme of "Cloud, IoT, and Network", even a small real sensor feed is likely to set a team apart from rivals who simulate their data. Keep the hardware scope small. See [IoT and network](/docs/research/iot-and-network.md).
- **Evidence:** campus navigation, chatbots, AI tutors, attendance, mental health and food waste are heavily represented on Devpost, appear in this hackathon's suggested ideas, and featured in GDGoC APU workshops. **Inference:** these will be crowded at APU. If a team picks one, it needs a sharp angle, as EqualLens and TrustLens had.
- **Evidence:** lecturer dashboards (35), classroom occupancy (28) and helpdesk tickets (60) have few Devpost matches. Campus energy (410) and study-space occupancy (216 `occupancy sensor` matches) are more common. **Inference:** live sensor data turned into a forecast or decision (Track 3) or into an automated workflow (Track 2) is a thinner field than Track 1 apps. Occupancy needs a decision layer on top to stand out.
- **Evidence:** winners chain several Google products into one pipeline, and seven of the 10 Solution Challenge winners used Flutter and Firebase. **Inference:** Gemini plus one data store and one hosted backend is enough. Show each product doing a distinct job in the architecture diagram. See [Google tools](/docs/research/google-tools.md).
- **Evidence:** measured validation is rare in public write-ups, and it stands out where present (Gaze Link's 30-person test). **Inference:** even five to ten APU users testing the prototype this week, with numbers in the deck, is a cheap advantage.
- **Evidence:** winners add trust and human-in-the-loop features (expert review, crowdsourced verification, bias checks, privacy redaction). **Inference:** include one such feature and name it in the deck. It also covers responsible-AI questions.
- **Evidence:** KitaHack's preliminary round appears to judge video, documentation and code, much like this hackathon's 11–14 November assessment. **Inference:** the README, deck and demo link probably decide who reaches the top 5, so polish them as carefully as the code.
- **Evidence:** none of the winners found here involved NTT DATA; its 2026 focus is in [judging and pitching](/docs/research/judging-and-pitching.md#ntt-data). Maybank's sponsor-set problem was rewarded with an enterprise workflow tool (DevSecOps). **Inference:** a Track 2 enterprise-style workflow, such as IT support or document routing, may suit a corporate sponsor's judges. This is unverified.
